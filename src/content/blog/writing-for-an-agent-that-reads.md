We used to write meeting notes for our future selves. Bullet points. Implied context. The kind of shorthand that makes sense if you were there.

Then we started feeding the same folder to an AI agent. The agent didn't understand a word of it. So we changed how we wrote.

## The rules that stuck

- **Lead with the topic.** The first line of every note says what the note is about. The agent reads top to bottom.
- **Spell out the acronyms once.** Just once. Then use them freely.
- **Date everything.** "Last week" means nothing six weeks later. Real dates make the agent's summaries accurate.
- **Use real headings.** `##` headings, not bold lines. The agent uses them as structure.
- **Don't fear length.** Short notes are fine. Long notes are also fine. Vague notes are not.

## What we noticed

After two weeks, the agent's drafts started sounding like us. Not magic, just consequence. Better input, better output.

And here's the side effect we didn't expect: our human reviewers started liking the notes more, too. Turns out, a note that an agent can follow is a note a teammate can follow.
